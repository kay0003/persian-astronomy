# PERSIAN ASTRONOMY: 22 SLIDES READY-TO-USE CONTENT

## SLIDE 1 - TITLE SLIDE
**Title:** Persian Astronomy
**Subtitle:** Stars, Science & Civilization — A Journey Through the Sky of Ancient & Medieval Persia
**Image:** Milky Way over desert

## SLIDE 2 - WHY PERSIA?
**Bullet Points:**
• One of the oldest continuous civilizations (3000+ years)
• Crossroads of trade, religion, and science
• Astronomy tied to calendar, agriculture, and religion
**Image:** Achaemenid Empire map

## SLIDE 3 - ZOROASTRIANISM & THE SKY
**Bullet Points:**
• Ancient Persians saw stars as sacred beings
• 4 royal stars guarded the 4 directions — Tishtrya linked to Sirius
• Fire temples aligned with celestial events
**Image:** Zoroastrian Fire Temple, Yazd

## SLIDE 4 - SACRED SYMBOL: FARAVAHAR
**Text:** Representing the spirit and Zoroastrian cosmic order
**Image:** Faravahar symbol

## SLIDE 5 - THE PERSIAN CALENDAR
**Bullet Points:**
• Solar Hijri calendar — one of the most accurate solar calendars ever devised
• Nowruz (New Year) precisely timed to the spring equinox — still used today!
**Image:** Nowruz Haft-Sin table

## SLIDE 6 - OMAR KHAYYAM (1048–1131)
**Bullet Points:**
• Poet AND mathematician-astronomer
• Reformed the Persian (Jalali) calendar — more accurate than the Gregorian calendar
• Worked at the Isfahan observatory
**Image:** Omar Khayyam statue

## SLIDE 7 - NASIR AL-DIN AL-TUSI (1201–1274)
**Bullet Points:**
• Founded the great Maragheh Observatory
• Invented the "Tusi Couple" — later echoed in Copernicus' work
• Compiled major star catalogs
**Image:** Nasir al-Din al-Tusi portrait (Artistic/imagined — no real historical depiction survives)

## SLIDE 8 - THE TUSI COUPLE
**Text:** A geometric device explaining planetary motion — an idea later echoed by Copernicus
**Image:** Tusi couple diagram

## SLIDE 9 - THE MARAGHEH OBSERVATORY
**Bullet Points:**
• Built in 1259 CE, scholars from Persia, China & beyond
• Library of 400,000+ books
• Inspired later observatories across Asia and possibly Europe
**Image:** Maragheh Observatory ruins

## SLIDE 10 - AL-BIRUNI (973–1048)
**Bullet Points:**
• Calculated Earth's radius with striking accuracy
• Discussed Earth possibly orbiting the Sun — centuries before Copernicus
• Wrote on latitude, longitude, eclipses, star positions
**Image:** Al-Biruni portrait

## SLIDE 11 - THE ASTROLABE
**Text:** A beautiful brass instrument refined by Persian scientists — used for navigation, timekeeping, and mapping stars.
**Image:** Persian astrolabe

## SLIDE 12 - SKY-ALIGNED ARCHITECTURE: PERSEPOLIS
**Text:** Columns and gates aligned with solstice sunrise
**Image:** Persepolis ruins

## SLIDE 13 - CHOGHA ZANBIL ZIGGURAT
**Text:** One of the few surviving ziggurats outside Mesopotamia — UNESCO World Heritage Site
**Image:** Chogha Zanbil ziggurat

## SLIDE 14 - LEGACY TODAY
**Text:** Persian astronomy terms passed into Arabic → Latin → English. Iran continues astronomy research today.
**Image:** Milky Way over desert (reuse from Slide 1)
